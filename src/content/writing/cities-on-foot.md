I've long believed that walking/running is the best way to truly experience a city. Here is why.

## Cities Are Smaller Than They Feel

Modern cities are dense, which means the neighborhoods and landmarks you actually care about are usually packed close together. A 5K or 10K route can cover a surprisingly large chunk of a city’s core. At an average walking pace of 5 km/h or a running pace of 10 km/h, a 10K takes one to two hours, long enough to cross half a dozen neighborhoods. You’re fully in the middle of the city, moving just fast enough to cover real ground and slow enough for serendipity to happen.

It’s always fun seeing how many different vibes I can pass through in a single 10K:

In Tokyo, [this 10K route](https://www.strava.com/activities/10330760758) weaves from chic, boutique Ebisu into young, bustling Shibuya and elegant Omotesando, past Meiji Jingu Gaien carpeted in yellow ginkgo leaves, and down through understated, restrained Minami-Aoyama into quiet, international Hiroo.

Back in my hometown of Guangzhou, [this 10K run along the Pearl River](https://www.strava.com/activities/10261533940) starts in earthy, lived-in Yuancun, passes newly minted boomtown Liede, cuts straight across the glass-and-steel CBD of Zhujiang New Town, and turns around on quiet, exclusive Ersha Island.

## High Information Bandwidth

When you take a subway, bus, or taxi, you’re traveling inside a sealed box—moving from point A to point B while skipping everything in between. On foot, the information bandwidth is huge. You are forced to pay attention to the streets, the people, and the subtle transitions between neighborhoods. You see, hear, smell, and feel the city in real time.

That sensory immersion creates room for serendipity that you just can’t plan on an itinerary. A few moments I only caught because I was out running/walking:

In Tokyo, on [a morning run starting out from Ikebukuro](https://www.strava.com/activities/9084302340), I picked a random direction and stumbled into Mejiro. Just a short jog from Ikebukuro’s neon and station crowds, Mejiro felt like another world—narrow streets lined with low-rise houses, potted plants by the front doors, and quiet neighborhood alleys.

Another time in Tokyo, on [December 15, 2023—an absurdly warm winter night](https://x.com/extremetemps/status/1735828300633903154?s=20)—I went for [a night run along the Yamanote Line](https://www.strava.com/activities/10384090145). December nights there usually sit around 5°C, but that night the temperature actually climbed as it got later, going from 18°C at 10 PM to 20°C by 3 AM. Running past glowing Christmas lights and holiday music in warm, summer-like air felt completely surreal.

In Hawaii, [this run](https://www.strava.com/activities/14583822282) made me realize just how close Diamond Head actually is to Waikiki (less than 5 km round-trip to the base of the crater). Along the way, I passed small oceanfront parks filled with morning yoga groups and found a narrow seaside path connecting Makalei and Leahi Beach Parks.

In Seoul, [looping around Yeouido on foot](https://www.strava.com/activities/19732140447) showed me how seamlessly Seoul pairs its well-built Han River running and cycling paths with Yeouido Park—a small Central Park-like green space embedded in the CBD.

## Earning Your Appetite

Part of the joy of visiting any city is eating your way through it. When you only have a short window to explore, you naturally want to sample as much as you can—which usually means eating far more than you would at home. Getting out on foot beforehand doesn’t just connect you to the city—it creates a welcome calorie deficit, so you sit down at the table hungry and ready to enjoy every bite.

## Low Friction

Running is pure freedom, all I need is a pair of shoes. It can be at dawn before the city wakes up, on a restless summer night, or on an unhurried afternoon. There are no constraints of time or place: no right moment, no specific venue, no special gear needed. I can go from staring at a screen to out the door in two minutes, with no booking, no transit, and no plan.

## My Favorite Hack: Turn Jet Lag Into Exploration Time

Whenever I fly back to Asia from the US, jet lag wakes me up at an ungodly hour, like 4:00 AM, long before any shops, cafes, or attractions open. Instead of staring at the ceiling, I lace up my shoes and go for a run while the city is still asleep, then follow it up with a warm bath and a proper, unhurried local breakfast. At home, my morning routine is usually just two eggs and a coffee; on the road, a sunrise run lets me enjoy a full feast before the rest of the city even wakes up. It’s easily my favorite way to start a trip.
